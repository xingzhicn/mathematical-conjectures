# Black-box polynomial identity testing

## Target

Fix a finite field $F$ and exponents $a,d>0$. Construct a deterministic $2^{n^{o(1)}}$-size hitting set for nonzero $n$-variate formal polynomials of degree at most $n^d$ computed by division-free circuits of size at most $n^a$. Generation time should obey the same asymptotic bound. In the finite-field model the black box must evaluate the polynomial over an explicitly represented extension field. This target is for exact circuits. Border circuits require a separate hitting statement.

## Existing derivation

Assume the cited superpolynomial lower bound for the directed Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial, with logarithmic circuit complexity $\Omega((\log N)^2/(\log\log N)^2)$. Pad this multilinear polynomial to $m$ variables and use the graphs of low-degree univariate polynomials over a prime field of order $m$ as a Nisan–Wigderson design. Taking $m=\exp(O(\sqrt{\log n}\log\log n))$ with sufficiently large constant, a seed of length $m^2$, and a grid larger than the composite's degree yields the query count $2^{2^{O(\sqrt{\log n}\log\log n)}}$. Each point can be generated by enumerating Hamiltonian cycles. [Hamiltonian-cycle lower bound][hc] · [Kabanets–Impagliazzo](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00037-004-0182-6)

In the hybrid argument a missed target yields a polynomial $R(X,Y)$ with a polynomial-size circuit and a root $Y=h(X)$, where $h$ is the hard source. If the multiplicity $e$ of $Y-h(X)$ is invertible in $F$, its Hasse derivative of order $e-1$ has a simple root at $h$. Truncated-series lifting then reconstructs a polynomial-size circuit for $h$. This covers characteristic zero and, for fixed characteristic $p$, targets of individual degree below $p$. These conclusions use the cited hardness as an input. In characteristic $p$, $R=(Y-h)^p$ makes the simple-root step fail. The first missing step is a paid reconstruction when $p\mid e$, or a different hybrid for which this case can be excluded.

## Direct geometric hitting set

For a separate restricted model, take degree-four set-multilinear forms whose coefficient tensor is a sum of two tensors with flattening rank at most $R$ across $12\mid34$ and $14\mid23$, respectively. For $\ell$ coordinates of dimension $d$ per site, one can form $(d^4-1)^\ell$ product evaluations from local tests whose common kernel in one coordinate consists of the crossing pair of equality wires of dimension $d$. A Euclidean price comparison excludes this kernel from the two-partition class for $R=d^\rho$, fixed $\rho<\sqrt2$, and sufficiently large $d$. Local contractions preserve the two rank bounds, which propagates the exclusion across the $\ell$ coordinates. The query count is smaller than the $d^{4\ell}$ coefficient queries of full interpolation. An extension would construct a larger local kernel whose nonzero tensors all satisfy a uniform price separation from the tested class. [Price calculus][prices]
