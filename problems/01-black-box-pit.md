# Black-box polynomial identity testing

## Target

Fix a finite field $F$ and exponents $a,d>0$. Construct a deterministic $2^{n^{o(1)}}$-size hitting set for nonzero $n$-variate formal polynomials of degree at most $n^d$ computed by division-free circuits of size at most $n^a$. Generation time should obey the same asymptotic bound. In the finite-field model the black box must evaluate the polynomial over an explicitly represented extension field. This target is for exact circuits. Border circuits require a separate hitting statement.

## Existing derivation

Assume the cited superpolynomial lower bound for the directed Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial, with logarithmic circuit complexity $\Omega((\log N)^2/(\log\log N)^2)$. Pad this multilinear polynomial to $m$ variables and use the graphs of low-degree univariate polynomials over a prime field of order $m$ as a Nisan–Wigderson design. Taking $m=\exp(O(\sqrt{\log n}\log\log n))$ with sufficiently large constant, a seed of length $m^2$, and a grid larger than the composite's degree yields the query count $2^{2^{O(\sqrt{\log n}\log\log n)}}$. Each point can be generated by enumerating Hamiltonian cycles. [Hamiltonian-cycle lower bound][hc] · [Kabanets–Impagliazzo](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00037-004-0182-6)

In the hybrid argument a missed target yields a polynomial $R(X,Y)$ with a polynomial-size circuit and a root $Y=h(X)$, where $h$ is the hard source. If the multiplicity $e$ of $Y-h(X)$ is invertible in $F$, its Hasse derivative of order $e-1$ has a simple root at $h$. Truncated-series lifting then reconstructs a polynomial-size circuit for $h$. This covers characteristic zero and, for fixed characteristic $p$, targets of individual degree below $p$. These conclusions use the cited hardness as an input. In characteristic $p$, $R=(Y-h)^p$ makes the simple-root step fail. The first missing step is a paid reconstruction when $p\mid e$, or a different hybrid for which this case can be excluded.

## Direct geometric hitting set

For a separate restricted model, take degree-four set-multilinear forms whose coefficient tensor is a sum of two tensors with flattening rank at most $R$ across $12\mid34$ and $14\mid23$, respectively. For $\ell$ coordinates of dimension $d$ per site, one can form $(d^4-1)^\ell$ product evaluations from local tests whose common kernel in one coordinate consists of the crossing pair of equality wires of dimension $d$. A Euclidean price comparison excludes this kernel from the two-partition class for $R=d^\rho$, fixed $\rho<\sqrt2$, and sufficiently large $d$. Local contractions preserve the two rank bounds, which propagates the exclusion across the $\ell$ coordinates. The query count is smaller than the $d^{4\ell}$ coefficient queries of full interpolation. The following construction enlarges the local kernel while keeping a uniform geometric lower bound on its nonzero elements. [Price calculus][prices]

### A larger query kernel from a linear code

Let $C\subset F^d$ be an $h$-dimensional linear code with minimum Hamming distance $s$. Use the four-site kernel
$$
K_C=\left\{\left(\sum_i c_i e_i^{(1)}\otimes e_i^{(3)}\right)
\otimes\left(\sum_j e_j^{(2)}\otimes e_j^{(4)}\right):c\in C\right\}.
$$
Every nonzero word has at least $s$ nonzero entries. Select $s$ of them, rescale on site 1, and truncate the second wire to $s$ entries. This restricts the tensor to two crossing equality wires of dimension $s$. The same geometric exclusion therefore works whenever the two partition ranks are bounded by $R<r_s$, where $r_s$ is the crossing rank of that wire pair.

The annihilator of $K_C$ has a basis of legal product evaluations. On sites 1 and 3, take all off-diagonal coordinate evaluations and the products $\mathbf 1\otimes w$ for a basis of $C^\perp$. They annihilate the first wire family and are independent, as seen by reading off the diagonal. Extend them to a product basis by adding $h$ diagonal coordinate evaluations. On sites 2 and 4, do the same with the repetition code, which needs one additional coordinate evaluation. In the product of the two extended bases, discard the $h$ pairs in which both evaluations are additional. The remaining $d^4-h$ evaluations are independent and annihilate $K_C$, so dimension gives equality. Iterating by local contractions gives $(d^4-h)^\ell$ queries.

For $|F|\ge d$, evaluate polynomials of degree less than $h=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$ at $d$ distinct points. The root bound gives $s=d-h+1$. Hence for each fixed $\rho<\sqrt 2$ the geometric exclusion still applies to $R\le d^\rho$ for sufficiently large $d$, by the cited sharp crossing exponent. This improves the one-direction kernel within this construction. Its complexity relative to other hitting sets for the same class is open here.

### A smaller hitting set from two ordinary matrix tests

Write $N=d^\ell$ for the dimension at one site and $r=\min(2R,N)$. Contract sites 1 and 3 against any two vectors. Every rank-one term on either allowed partition becomes a rank-one matrix on sites 2 and 4. The remaining matrix therefore has rank at most $r$. The same observation applies with the two opposite pairs exchanged.

Here is a product hitting set $\mathcal H$ for matrices of rank at most $r$. Work over an evaluation field containing $2N-1$ distinct elements and an element $\alpha$ of multiplicative order at least $N$. Write $v(t)=(1,t,\ldots,t^{N-1})$. Use the pairs $(v(t),v(\alpha^j t))$ for $0\le j<r$ and $2N-1$ different values of $t$. If all evaluations vanish, interpolation shows that the entries on each antidiagonal have their first $r$ Vandermonde moments zero, with distinct nodes $\alpha^i$ indexed by column. Every nonempty antidiagonal must then contain at least $r+1$ nonzeros. On the first nonempty antidiagonal, choose those row and column indices, with columns in reverse order. The resulting triangular minor has nonzero diagonal, contradicting the rank bound. Coordinate evaluations also work, so take the smaller set of size $L=\min(N^2,r(2N-1))$. This is the matrix low-rank hitting-set method of [Forbes and Shpilka](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.0663).

Place one copy of $\mathcal H$ on 1 and 3 and a second on 2 and 4. If a form vanishes on the resulting $L^2$ queries, fix any tested pair on 1 and 3. The residual matrix is zero by the first hitting property. Consequently every pair on 2 and 4, including every coordinate pair, gives zero against every tested pair on 1 and 3. Applying the hitting property on the latter sites shows that every coefficient vanishes. This gives $O(R^2N^2)$ deterministic product evaluations. One may pass to an explicit finite extension for the required evaluation elements. For fixed $d$ and admissible fixed $R$, this has exponent two in $N$, below that of the code-based geometric construction.

At least $RN^2$ scalar queries are necessary for a fixed hitting set when $1\le R\le N^2$. Indeed the first partition alone contains the vector space of matrices supported on $R$ prescribed rows of its $N^2$-by-$N^2$ flattening. The dimension argument applies when coefficients and evaluations lie in the same field. If evaluations use an extension of degree $e$ over the coefficient field, it gives the weaker lower bound $\lceil RN^2/e\rceil$. The remaining gap in the sparse-rank range is one factor $R$. A competitive role for geometry here would be to couple the two opposite-pair tests and remove that factor.

Over an algebraically closed field, generic legal product evaluations already attain that order. Each of the two flattening summands is specified by $2RN^2$ scalar factor entries, so the projective closure of the nonzero class has dimension at most $4RN^2-1$. For each nonzero form in it, the set of vanishing single product queries in affine $4N$-space is a hypersurface. The incidence variety of forms vanishing on $t$ queries has dimension at most $4RN^2-1+t(4N-1)$. At $t=4RN^2$ this is smaller than the dimension $4Nt$ of query tuples. An explicit construction of that size, with efficient generation over the intended field, is the outstanding task.

### What the inseparable hybrid still reconstructs

In characteristic $p$, write the root multiplicity as $e=bm$ with $b=p^{v_p(e)}$ and $p\nmid m$. Expand the nonzero hybrid by its exponent of $Y$ modulo $b$,
$$
R(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}Y^jR_j(X,Y^b).
$$
The free basis $1,Y,\ldots,Y^{b-1}$ over $F[X,Y^b]$ and the identity $(Y-h)^b=Y^b-h^b$ show that $(Z-h^b)^m$ divides every $R_j(X,Z)$. Some nonzero $R_j$ has multiplicity exactly $m$ there, since otherwise $(Y-h)^{b(m+1)}$ would divide $R$. Its Hasse derivative in $Z$ of order $m-1$ has a simple root at $h^b$. Coefficient interpolation in $Y$ constructs this $R_j$ and its derivative with polynomial overhead in $\deg_Y R$ over the algebraic closure. The same truncated-series lifting as in the separable case reconstructs a circuit for $h^b$ in size polynomial in the hybrid size and degrees, including $b\deg h$. Since $b\le\deg_Y R$, this is polynomial in the original hybrid parameters. Thus a uniform hardness bound for the relevant Frobenius powers of the Hamiltonian-cycle source would suffice. The cited lower bound for $h$ supplies the case $b=1$.
